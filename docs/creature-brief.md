# Creature brief

Agreed direction for how each faction's figures should look and move. The
`army-animation` skill builds from this file.

Everything here is written from unit names, keywords and stat lines, or from
direction given in conversation. Nothing is derived from photographs of the
printed cards — see the originality rule in the skill.

---

## Lizardmen

*Direction inferred from the unit list; not yet confirmed. Replace this note
once the shape has been agreed.*

The army list divides cleanly into three peoples and a menagerie, and the
names do most of the work:

| Unit | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Swarmling Warriors, Swarmling Bowmen | the small ones, many to a stand |
| Trog Warriors, Trog Spearmen, Trog Bowmen | the middleweights, squat and broad |
| Tyrant Warriors, Tyrant Spearmen | the elite, tall and upright |
| Hatchlings, Raptor Pack | beasts, running loose rather than in ranks |
| Triceratops Herd, Ancients | Large — heavy saurians, two or three to a stand |
| Tyrannosaurus Rex | Colossal — has its own sculpt already |

**Body plan.** Reptilian rather than humanoid-in-scales. That means a spine
carried closer to horizontal than a man's, a counterbalancing tail, a wedge
head with a snout rather than a ball, and digitigrade legs with a wide,
splayed stance.

**The top-down signature is the dorsal ridge and the tail.** From directly
overhead a pale ridge running the length of the spine, ending in a tail
swinging behind, says *reptile* before anything else registers. Every
lizardfolk breed carries both; it is what makes them read as one army.

**Proportions by breed.**

- *Swarmling* — small, lean, low to the ground, long tail. Crowded onto the
  stand, more of them than anything else fields.
- *Trog* — squat and broad, heavy through the shoulders, short thick tail.
  Toad-like in mass, still reptilian in outline.
- *Tyrant* — tall and upright, the most humanoid of the three, with the
  proudest crest. Fewer to a stand, and they should look like it.

**Palette.** The turf is dark yellow-green, so the bodies cannot be. Cool
slates and teals separate from it by hue *and* value; the pale bone ridge
and crest carry the contrast. Swarmlings run warmer and more ochre, which
also helps tell the three breeds apart at a glance.

**The heavy saurians.** A Large stand carries two or three animals, not a
rank, and each has to read on its own with no formation to help it. Each is
therefore built around one broad horizontal feature, because breadth is the
only currency a top-down camera accepts: the Triceratops is wide at the head,
with a pale bone frill fanned flat behind the skull; an Ancient is wide across
the shoulders, under a lichen-covered shell, with a crown of horns raked back
off it. That difference — wide in front versus wide amidships — is what tells
them apart at a glance.

They are also deliberately stubbier than the real animals. The band a stand
gives its figures is roughly two and a half times wider than it is deep, so
length costs size on the table and there is no room for it.

**Movement.** The tail is the animation, not the legs. A tail swinging
counter to the stride is visible from above where a leg cycle mostly is not.

## Orc Army

Modelled: Orc Axemen as the infantry archetype, Goblin Wolf Riders as the
cavalry archetype.

**Known problem.** The axemen's armour is `0x2f2a24`, which reads as a black
lump at stand scale against the dark turf. They need a lighter element —
shield face, helm, or weapon head — carrying real value contrast. See the
"Value contrast beats detail" section of the skill.
